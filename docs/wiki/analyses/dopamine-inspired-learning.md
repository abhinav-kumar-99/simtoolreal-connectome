# Dopamine-inspired online learning

Reward-modulated eligibility traces are a plausible alternative learning rule, but the current connectome actor has no native dopamine plasticity and lower update cost does not guarantee faster task learning.

## Biological evidence and boundary

In fly mushroom-body learning, dopaminergic signals modulate plasticity at specific circuit sites; dopamine is not a uniform command to increase every active connection. Different pathways mediate appetitive and aversive reinforcement, and timing matters. [Felsenberg et al., Nature 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature21716) demonstrate reward-memory re-evaluation involving distinct reinforcing dopamine populations and describe reward-associated depression of Kenyon-cell output to avoidance pathways. [Ueno et al., eLife 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5262376/) examine coincident activity, dopamine release and mushroom-body plasticity. These are biological associative-learning results, not demonstrations of cheap dexterous robot learning.

[Bellec et al., Nature Communications 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7367848/) derive eligibility propagation (e-prop): forward-computed local traces combined with learning signals approximate recurrent-network learning without backpropagation through time. Reward-based versions exist. Approximate neuron-specific credit signals are important; simply multiplying arbitrary Hebbian coactivity by positive reward is not equivalent to a policy gradient. The cited work concerns spiking networks (with additional derivations for other recurrent models), not this exact sparse tanh/gain/SAPG actor, and does not establish better sample efficiency here.

## Original PPO-only setup

`rl_games/rl_games/algos_torch/connectome_network_builder.py::_step` computes adapter drives, fixed signed recurrence with bounded learned gains, leak and tanh. It has no dopamine concentration, receptor dynamics, eligibility state, or online synaptic update. Parameter changes come from the PPO optimizer between rollouts. The compact manifest has no dopamine-labelled cells, although 53 labels are unclear and four missing; absence of a label alone is not proof about every cell's physiology. More importantly, chemical learning machinery and a mushroom-body learning circuit were not implemented. Adding a neuron labelled dopamine or stimulating current cells would not by itself enable learning.

The actor is anatomy-informed wiring with simplified dynamics and robot adapters, not a pretrained biological dexterity program with an intact biochemical learning system. A synthetic reward-modulated update does not require adding dopamine neurons or expanding the graph, but must be described as an engineered learning rule.

## Proposed first experiment, not implemented

Warm-start from an explicitly selected checkpoint; freeze recurrence, gains, input adapters, embeddings and normalization statistics for the first comparison. Train only the motor-to-action readout (135 x 29 weights plus 29 biases, 3,944 parameters) and a lightweight value predictor. Frozen input adapters are important: merely freezing the base recurrent weights does not eliminate recurrent backpropagation while input adapters still learn.

For a stochastic policy, let the teaching signal be the engineered TD error `delta_t = r_t + gamma * V(h_next) - V(h_t)`, with correct terminal handling. Maintain a fading eligibility trace based on the sampled action's log-probability gradient with respect to readout parameters, not unqualified neuron coactivity. Conceptually, `e_t = gamma * lambda * e_(t-1) + grad_theta log pi_theta(a_t | h_t)` and `theta += eta * delta_t * e_t`. The reward-prediction-error construction is an algorithmic dopamine analogy, not a claim that all fly dopamine neurons compute this equation. Signed errors mean better/worse than expected, not just larger reward equals more dopamine forever.

Because the recurrent feature generator is fixed, readout gradients require no backpropagation through recurrent state. Use on-policy exploration and small bounded updates; reset per-environment traces at episode boundaries and aggregate reward-times-trace products across environments (not a product of separately averaged rewards and traces). Existing mixed-exploration SAPG data cannot be reused indiscriminately without its policy/off-policy semantics. Value learning, normalization, log-standard-deviation adaptation and termination masks must be made explicit in a future YAML contract.

A later, riskier variant could update gains or adapters through derived rate-network eligibility traces and approximate feedback signals. Keep support/sign/gain constraints and benchmark stability. This is substantially more implementation work than changing a reward coefficient. Direct edge plasticity is not required for the first experiment. At 12,288 environments, even one FP32 trace per 33,720 edges occupies about 1.54 GiB before adapter traces and other state; local learning is not automatically cheap on a GPU.

## From-scratch eligibility design (historical proposal)

The user also asked about starting without a trained checkpoint. This is a distinct proposal from the readout-only warm start: initialize sensory/descending adapters and the motor readout with small nonzero random weights, neuron gains at one, recurrent state/traces at zero, and a small value predictor from scratch. Preserve the 1,952 IDs, 33,720 base connections, sign constraints and fixed leak/bias settings. The wiring is the prior; there is no initial robot skill to assume. Learning only the output of randomly frozen adapters would be a restricted reservoir baseline, not an equivalent replacement for current adapters-plus-gains training.

Train sensory and descending adapters, motor readout and bounded incoming/outgoing gains with online approximate policy eligibility. A first trainer can use one explicitly defined on-policy Gaussian exploration distribution (and fixed exploration-conditioning code) rather than silently inheriting SAPG's mixed-policy reuse. Any change from six exploration blocks needs labeling as an algorithm change and a matched baseline, not just a faster optimizer. Learn a lightweight value predictor using TD errors; its own feedforward gradients need not involve recurrent BPTT.

At each robot transition:

1. Compute one normal actor recurrent step, motor action mean, and sampled action; do not change the forward graph or increase recurrent steps.
2. Update local neuron/parameter sensitivity traces during the forward calculation. For an input-adapter weight, the direct contribution contains its robot feature, destination tanh slope and leak. Incoming-gain contributions contain that destination's recurrent input; outgoing gains affect every outgoing edge and require aggregation across downstream targets, not one arbitrary coactivity number. Derivatives must include the bounded raw-to-gain parameterization.
3. Convert local traces into approximate **action-credit** signals. For the current unsquashed Gaussian mean, the action score is `(sampled_action - mean) / variance`; multiply it by motor features for exact direct readout credit. Handle clipping/squashing according to the actual policy distribution. Non-motor cells lack direct readout weights, so they require a specified feedback estimator, for example a fixed signed projection of the action-score vector to neuron-specific learning signals. This is an auxiliary training-only signal, not a new forward connectome edge. Its quality, scale and temporal alignment are open implementation choices.
4. Accumulate discounted action-credit traces: `Z_theta,t = gamma * lambda * Z_theta,t-1 + qhat_theta,t`, where `qhat` is the online estimate of action log-probability sensitivity. Distinguish this delayed-reward trace from the local neural-dynamics trace in step 2. Neither trace is a proof of causal necessity.
5. Observe reward and the next state, compute `delta_t = r_t + gamma * V(next) - V(current)` with correct true-terminal/time-limit handling, and apply a small shared update proportional to the environment average of `delta_t * Z_theta,t`. Value updates are separate. Updates can be batched over a few fresh transitions for GPU efficiency without storing an autograd-unrolled recurrent graph or running repeated PPO replay epochs. Clear episode traces before using reset observations for a new episode.

This is an **e-prop-inspired rate-network actor-critic design**, not a derived exact gradient for this code and not a drop-in implementation of the spiking-neuron paper. Local eligibility addresses temporal bookkeeping; neuron-specific feedback addresses spatial credit. Their combination replaces the retrospective gradient propagation in BPTT with an approximation. Using only a scalar reward times pre/post coactivity would omit the action-specific credit mechanism and can reinforce irrelevant activity. A neuron changing internal state need not have influenced the sampled action.

For instance, a sampled finger action might improve object alignment. Positive TD error reinforces signed traces associated with making that action more likely in the current context; it does not increase all weights or gains. When the motion worsens the outcome relative to expectation, the sign reverses. Useful improvement from scratch still needs exploration and informative rewards. Start with the existing shaped task reward for comparability, not terminal success alone; a trace cannot learn an undiscovered successful behavior without experience. Trace decay, feedback estimator, per-group learning rates, exploration scale, update cadence, clipping, value architecture and reset semantics need explicit future YAML settings and gradient/ablation tests. No numeric setting or performance claim is validated yet.

## Why deep RL usually retains backpropagation, and what is special here

Eligibility methods are not absent from RL: TD(lambda), SARSA(lambda) and actor-critic traces are established approaches. Modern advantage estimators also use related temporal-credit ideas. [Schulman et al., GAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02438) explicitly describe their estimator as analogous to TD(lambda). Our `a2c_common.py::discount_values` computes `adv_t = delta_t + gamma * tau * nonterminal * adv_next`; the compact run's resolved YAML has gamma 0.99, tau (lambda) 0.95, horizon 16 and sequence length 16. This forward-view reward-credit computation does not implement local parameter eligibility or eliminate BPTT. The novel proposal is the latter, not adding delayed-reward credit for the first time.

Backpropagation differentiates the actual computational graph within the chosen truncation window, including indirect recurrent paths. Local online rules need approximate spatial feedback, or pay for much more expensive exact recurrent sensitivity tracking. Longer reward traces trade temporal reach against noise, and changing representations/policies complicate the meaning of old traces. Purely local coactivity is insufficient evidence of behavioral responsibility. [Bellec et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17236-y) explicitly report slower learning than BPTT in their discussion of e-prop, despite approaching its performance in their studied tasks; biological plausibility is not a general sample-efficiency advantage.

[The PPO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347) motivates multiple minibatch optimization epochs from collected data and evaluates a favorable balance of sample complexity, implementation simplicity and wall time. PPO clipping is a practical update-control mechanism, not a universal monotonic-improvement guarantee. An online trace learner can incorporate its own stabilization and off-policy corrections, but those do not come for free. Batched autograd uses mature GPU primitives; many per-step trace operations and per-environment trace storage can offset nominal backward-pass savings. These are engineering and statistical tradeoffs, not proof that no online method can win.

Reasons this actor is a useful test bed, **not established advantages over PPO**:

- Sparse fixed recurrence with 33,720 edges and 3,904 trainable incoming/outgoing gain scalars is more constrained than learning a dense recurrent matrix. Gain sharing can reduce the adaptation search space, but constrains PPO identically and does not make exact outgoing-gain eligibility a one-scalar local operation.
- Known input/output populations make local sensitivity bookkeeping and targeted feedback experiments tractable. Conversely, actions are read from only 135 of 1,952 cells; most internal cells need indirect credit. Sparsity and a restricted motor readout do not automatically improve random-feedback alignment.
- The anatomical topology may supply useful sensorimotor dynamics so coarse adjustment is sufficient. This remains the substantive biological hypothesis. The current tanh dynamics, global normalization, transmitter assumptions and random cross-species adapters do not establish an evolved executable robot controller or recovered dopamine/receptor learning rules.
- Structured numeric observations and shaped task reward avoid a large learned visual encoder in this actor, making the proposed local learner smaller. But 56,060 input-adapter weights still need to learn robot-to-circuit mappings from scratch, and contact-rich 29-action control remains difficult. These favorable input/reward conditions also help PPO.
- Online traces could be attractive for continual adaptation or much longer credit horizons without an autograd unroll. Current BPTT already uses only 16 steps, and the earlier measured update-time share was small; there is no demonstrated current throughput advantage. A longer configured trace is not automatically useful memory or reliable long-range credit.

The honest claim is that this setup makes a controlled eligibility experiment feasible, not that fly-derived wiring predicts it will outperform PPO. First compare the same actor/trainable groups, reward, critic capacity, exploration and seed cohort under PPO versus the new rule, recording both frame-matched and wall-time-matched learning. To test a connectome-specific benefit rather than a generic optimizer effect, compare both algorithms on biological and appropriately matched rewired graphs. That four-cell comparison would test whether the relative algorithm benefit depends on topology; it would not prove a dopamine mechanism. No experiment was launched by this discussion.

## Measured cost constraint

Read-only inspection of the running compact job's `train.log`, with 1,607 timing records available at inspection, found these means over its last 100 phases:

| Timed region | Seconds per phase |
| --- | ---: |
| Rollout collection, including policy inference | 2.40101 |
| PPO update region | 0.55143 |
| Total timed epoch | 2.95247 |

The regions are defined in `rl_games/rl_games/common/a2c_common.py` around `update_time_start`, `update_time_end` and the timing printout. This is a time-specific snapshot, including reset-dependent variation, not an isolated benchmark. Even eliminating the entire measured update region would save only about 18.7% of this timed epoch at unchanged sample count and rollout cost (approximately 1.23x throughput). A practical replacement still costs something and total wall time also includes work outside these regions. Much larger speedups require fewer interactions or faster rollout collection, not just removing BPTT.

Evaluate any future experiment against PPO using both equal environment-frame budgets and equal wall time, with matched initial checkpoint, seeds and task distribution, and frozen-policy mean-action evaluation. Compare fine tracking, perturbation recovery and contact/multijoint coordination, not merely reward or seconds per update. Readout-only learning restricts capacity and may learn more slowly or plateau. Neither dopamine-inspired rules nor e-prop currently justify a claim of reduced time-to-dexterity on this task. This discussion does not authorize replacing or stopping the ongoing runs.
