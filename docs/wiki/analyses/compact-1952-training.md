## Root cause: unbounded exploration log standard deviation

Correction: the saved `a2c_network.sigma` tensor is the Gaussian **log** standard deviation, despite its name. Negative saved entries are valid. `continuous_a2c_logstd` exponentiates this tensor before constructing the Gaussian. The failure message therefore did not establish a negative learned standard deviation; in this run it was produced after the exponentiated value became numerically invalid.

Both policies experienced the same exploration-distribution runaway. Their six coefficient-conditioned log-standard-deviation rows are unconstrained. The positive SAPG entropy terms subtract `entropy_coefficient * entropy` from the minimized PPO loss, so they continually push the higher-entropy rows upward unless the task-policy gradient counteracts them. The adaptive KL scheduler also raised the nominal `1e-4` actor learning rate as high as its hard maximum `1e-2`. The gains block-0 log-standard-deviation mean/max grew from 0.979/1.592 at 250 million frames to 84.800/86.463 at 2.25 billion; adapters-only grew from 1.054/1.501 to 86.393/87.497 by 3.25 billion. The corresponding standard deviations are approximately `exp(log_std)`. Float32 `exp` overflows near 88.7, sampled-action multiplication can overflow before that, and the existing KL calculation squares standard deviations and can overflow once `log_std` is only about 44.4.

The gains policy reached the dangerous range earlier because its PPO/KL trajectory drove the highest-entropy row upward much faster; the extra gain parameters changed that trajectory but were not themselves the failing operation. The adapters-only recurrence was exactly frozen and still developed the same action-distribution problem. All actor-core and critic tensors in the last recovery checkpoints are finite, and the exception occurs in Gaussian sampling after recurrence. This evidence does not implicate the Triton recurrent kernel or a connectome hidden-state overflow.

The old-timing compact contracts schedule eight actor optimizer calls per 196,608 fresh frames, twice the released policy's eight calls per 393,216 fresh frames. This doubles entropy-pressure opportunities per environment frame and is one reason matching only the released entropy coefficient `0.005` did not reproduce the release's training dynamics. At about one billion frames, the matched-release-timing 4,310-cell runs still had block-0 log-standard-deviation means of 1.308 (adapters) and 2.039 (gains), whereas the old-timing compact runs were already at 4.031 and 9.432. Architecture, seed and graph also differ, so this comparison isolates neither timing nor actor by itself.

Mixed-precision `GradScaler` prevented many corrupt gradients from being applied, but it is not a recovery mechanism: after the forward loss became non-finite it skipped optimizer steps while leaving the already extreme distribution parameter in place. This explains why the adapters process kept accumulating frames while its actor step counter stopped. Mean-action evaluation videos do not sample this distribution, so they can remain finite even when stochastic training is broken.

### Live entropy audit

TensorBoard inspection at frames 72,744,960 (gains) and 79,429,632 (adapters) confirms that entropy is rising. `losses/entropy` starts at 41.149 nats, the exact 29-dimensional diagonal-Gaussian entropy for log standard deviation zero, and reached 42.858/43.304. These imply aggregate geometric-mean standard deviations of approximately 1.061/1.077. The more revealing block-0 values reached 45.562/46.927 nats, corresponding to mean log standard deviations 0.152/0.199 and geometric-mean standard deviations 1.164/1.220. The latest frame-39,321,600 recovery checkpoints had maximum individual block-0 log standard deviations only 0.226/0.268. Thus the current distributions are finite and not near float32 overflow, although checkpoints lag live events.

An early increase is directly encouraged by the configured SAPG objective. Six blocks receive entropy coefficients `[0.0025, 0.0020, 0.0015, 0.0010, 0.0005, 0]`, obtained from `expl_reward_coef_scale: 0.005` times a linear 0.5-to-0 schedule. The minimized actor loss subtracts coefficient times raw Gaussian entropy. The live ordering broadly reflects this pressure: block 0 rises fastest, while block 4 has fallen below initialization. Block 5 has no direct entropy bonus but can still move through the PPO likelihood objective.

Continual unbounded growth is not a desirable convergence property. Gaussian differential entropy has no maximum here, action log standard deviations are unconstrained, and simulator actions are clipped after sampling. Large variance can therefore improve the entropy term while adding little useful executed exploration. The repaired KL only measures local policy change and makes invalid measurements reduce LR; it does not constrain accumulated drift from the initial policy. The current standard-schedule pattern also commonly observes high KL in mini-epoch 0 and low KL in mini-epoch 1, so a divide-by-1.5 followed by multiply-by-1.5 can cancel within a phase. At this snapshot, aggregate logged LR was 0.0003375 for both policies and neither had logged the 0.01 ceiling, so the ongoing entropy drift is not evidence that the actor is currently stuck at max LR.

The proper interpretation is therefore: rising entropy is expected early for the rewarded exploration members, but a persistent monotonic rise without stabilization is a warning because this exact unconstrained quantity drove the previous long-run failures. Reward also rose during this early window, so the logs do not yet establish that current exploration is harmful. Monitor block-specific entropy/log standard deviation, action clipping or saturation, reward/success, and invalid-KL/applied-step telemetry; stable KL alone is insufficient.

### Current MLP entropy comparison

An audit on 2026-09-14 after the eligibility jobs were stopped found both MLP PPO trainers alive and finite, but the KL-0.016 policy's exploration member was already operationally pathological. At approximately 1.34B frames, the KL-0.004 run's aggregate entropy was 46.86 nats and block-0 entropy was 53.3 nats. Its frame-1,297,612,800 recovery checkpoint had block-0 mean/max log standard deviation 0.411/0.580, corresponding to geometric-mean/maximum standard deviations 1.51/1.79. At approximately 1.12B frames, the KL-0.016 run's aggregate entropy was about 99.1 nats and block-0 entropy was about 375 nats. Its frame-1,101,004,800 recovery checkpoint had block-0 mean/max log standard deviation 11.373/12.108, corresponding to standard deviations about 87,000/181,000. Model tensors in both recovery checkpoints were finite. The issue is useful-action semantics well before numeric overflow: the environment executes a clipped action in `[-1, 1]`, so the KL-0.016 block-0 samples are effectively saturated endpoint commands.

The exact loss path explains the ordered growth. `continuous_a2c_logstd` exponentiates the actor output before constructing the Gaussian, so the network field named `sigma` is log standard deviation. With 29 actions, raw Gaussian entropy is `29 * 0.5 * log(2*pi*e) + sum(log_std)`, giving the observed 41.149-nat initialization. The six coefficient-conditioned rows receive fixed entropy coefficients `[0.0025, 0.0020, 0.0015, 0.0010, 0.0005, 0]`. `a2c_continuous.py` minimizes PPO loss minus coefficient times raw entropy, giving every positive-coefficient row a persistent direct upward log-standard-deviation gradient. The generic `entropy_coef` is zero but is not used in this mixed-exploration branch. Neither run has a target entropy, entropy-coefficient decay, log-standard-deviation bound, or clipping-aware entropy. The `fixed_sigma: coef_cond` name means state-independent within a coefficient row, not frozen; the `6 x 29` tensor is an ordinary trainable parameter in the actor Adam optimizer.

The KL scheduler limits only one update relative to the immediately preceding distribution, not accumulated distance from initialization. It also updates after each mini-epoch. In current snapshots, both jobs show the same cancellation pattern: mini-epoch 0 exceeds twice its threshold and divides LR by 1.5, while mini-epoch 1 falls below half its threshold and multiplies LR back by 1.5. The KL-0.016 run previously reached the `0.01` ceiling and a peak mini-epoch-0 KL above 44; the KL-0.004 run's recorded ceiling was approximately 0.00577. This strongly explains the difference between otherwise matched actors. It does not establish that the connectome representation is irrelevant to all learning dynamics, but the runaway parameter and its entropy gradient bypass recurrence.

This mechanism is SAPG-specific, not fly-specific. `SimToolRealLSTMAsymmetricSAPG` configures the same mixed-exploration objective, six coefficient-conditioned log-standard-deviation rows, Gaussian wrapper, optimizer and action clipping, so a normal LSTM trained under that profile can exhibit the same failure. Ordinary `SimToolRealLSTMAsymmetricPPO` instead uses one state-independent trainable log-standard-deviation vector, `expl_type: none`, and `entropy_coef: 0`; its variance can still move through PPO likelihood gradients but does not receive this fixed positive entropy push. A frozen connectome may provide a weaker counter-gradient through its mean policy than a flexible LSTM, but current logs do not isolate that hypothesis.

The immediate operational recommendation is to stop the KL-0.016 run; continuing it spends most block-0 exploration on clipping and risks the same eventual overflow as the earlier linear policies. The KL-0.004 run is not yet numerically extreme, but an unbounded 100B continuation is not protected. A durable fresh experiment should (1) bound or freeze log standard deviations per block, preferably with YAML-owned limits/targets; (2) replace raw clipped-Gaussian entropy with a bounded-action distribution such as a tanh-squashed Gaussian whose log probability and entropy match executed actions; (3) decay or target the SAPG exploration coefficients rather than relying on the unrelated generic entropy scheduler; (4) prevent per-mini-epoch KL LR decisions from canceling, or stop a PPO update when its KL budget is exceeded; and (5) log per-block log-standard-deviation min/mean/max and sampled/executed clipping fraction with an automatic stop threshold. Lowering the KL target, maximum LR or entropy scale can slow the drift, but none alone creates a finite optimum.

The selected replacement implements item (2) without item (1): at the user's direction its latent Gaussian log standard deviation remains completely unclamped. The bounded transformed entropy itself has a finite maximum, so this experiment tests whether aligning the optimized distribution with executed actions is sufficient without imposing a variance limit.

### Clipping correctness boundary

The implementation is internally consistent if the stochastic policy action is defined as an unbounded latent variable followed by a deterministic clip. Rollout collection stores the raw Gaussian sample and its raw Gaussian negative log probability before `ContinuousA2CBase.preprocess_actions` clips it to `[-1, 1]`; PPO later computes its likelihood ratio from that same raw sample. The task base applies a second configurable clamp before physics, which is a safety backstop rather than the source of the entropy problem. Computing an ordinary Gaussian log probability at the already clipped value would be incorrect because hard clipping maps an interval of latent samples to point mass at each endpoint.

The implementation is not aligned with an interpretation of entropy as diversity in commands executed by the simulator. Entropy and KL are computed for the raw Gaussian, whereas reward depends on the clipped pushforward distribution. Once variance is large, increasing it changes raw entropy without materially enriching executed commands; probability instead accumulates at `-1` and `+1`. The existing bounds loss constrains only the action mean outside a soft range of `[-1.1, 1.1]` and does not constrain log standard deviation. A principled bounded-action replacement must transform both sampling and likelihood consistently, for example a tanh-squashed Gaussian with its log-Jacobian correction, rather than merely moving the current clamp before the log-probability calculation.

### Interpretation of KL and the baseline comparison

Raw-event audit of the stopped pre-repair runs (all 11,574 gains and 17,066 adapters PPO records, without TensorBoard display sampling): gains `info/kl` becomes permanently NaN at frame 1,765,343,232, and adapters at 2,670,329,856. The remaining 2,595 and 3,484 logged LR values are constant at 0.01 and 0.006666667 respectively. First actor-loss NaNs arrive much later, at 2,259,812,352 and 3,190,554,624 (intermittent initially). Thus KL feedback failed roughly half a billion frames before actor loss failed. Earlier LR decreases are present: gains 0.0044444446 to 0.002962963 after logged KL 0.35763 at 110,690,304; adapters 0.0005852766 to 0.0003901844 after KL 0.47862 at 101,646,336. These examples are consistent with an active scheduler, not a complete replay of its decisions.

Historical aggregate logging prevented an exact per-decision audit: the scheduler was called after each of two mini-epochs, but `info/kl` was their mean and `info/last_lr` was the LR returned by the final minibatch before the second scheduler call. A reduction after one mini-epoch could be offset by an increase after the other. Near numerical failure the old computation also produced impossible negative KL values (adapters -0.12280 at frame 2,669,543,424 with LR 0.01), which satisfied the scheduler's increase condition; NaN comparisons left LR unchanged. The current implementation logs each mini-epoch and maps negative/non-finite KL to an LR reduction, but 0.004/0.016 remains an adaptive trigger rather than a hard limit or early-stop gate.

KL measures the conditional action distributions, summed over the same 29 action dimensions and averaged over training samples; it is not divided by actor parameter count. Architecture changes the parameter-to-distribution Jacobian, gradients, state visitation and the adaptive learning-rate trajectory, but does not change KL units. A restricted mean actor with the same freely learned 6-by-29 log-standard-deviation table can have a different balance between task and entropy gradients. Whether this balance caused the initial divergence needs gradient measurements or a matched intervention; the checkpoints alone do not establish it.

The environment clamps sampled actions to [-1, 1] before rescaling, while the entropy bonus uses the unclipped Gaussian. At large variance the executed commands approach endpoint saturation, so further variance growth can keep increasing the entropy bonus while adding little useful exploration. KL constrains local distribution changes only: increasing every log standard deviation by 0.01 yields approximately 0.002881 exact KL across 29 dimensions irrespective of its starting magnitude. Repeating such small changes does not bound total variance. The historical float32 implementation returned NaN for log standard deviations 45 and 45.01 because it squared their exponentials despite that small exact KL. The repaired implementation uses float64 scale ratios and normalized mean differences and returns the finite local KL, but this arithmetic fix still cannot bound cumulative variance growth.

The release's successful endpoint does not establish that the authors never encountered instability, and available artifacts do not provide their per-block sigma/gradient history. The claim that smaller parameter count or doubled update density definitively caused the runaway is therefore too strong. The observed variance growth and numerical failure are established; architecture-dependent gradient balance, action clipping, entropy pressure and update density provide plausible interacting causes. A controlled comparison needs the same graph, initialization and task with only update timing changed, plus per-block log standard deviation, clipping fraction, stable KL, entropy/task gradient contributions and applied optimizer-step telemetry.
